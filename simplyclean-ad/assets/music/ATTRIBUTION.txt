Exact attribution string to use (the author's own requested format, quoted
verbatim from the track page):

  'Catalyst' by Scott Buckley - released under CC-BY 4.0. www.scottbuckley.com.au

Where to put it: the author's stated requirement is a credit line, with a link
to the licence, in the video description / accompanying credits text:
  "Important info for YouTube users: Credits must be provided in the video
   description (not in the video itself). If you don't do this, you will
   likely get a copyright claim on the video."
So: put the string above in the ad's description/metadata on every platform it
is published on (YouTube, LinkedIn, the site, etc.). A small end-card credit is
also worth adding for safety, but the author asks for the description, not the
video itself.

IMPORTANT CAVEAT — read before using this in paid/broadcast placement.
Quoted from the author's licence page, verbatim:
  "What if you are working on a project which cannot provide attribution (for
   instance, a TV advertisement)? In this instance, the CC-BY license does not
   apply, and you will need to buy a license."
If this ad is ever placed somewhere a credit line genuinely cannot appear
(broadcast TV, some paid ad placements), this free licence does not cover that
use and a paid licence from the composer would be required. For online/web
placement where the description can carry the credit, CC BY 4.0 applies.

Also quoted from the same page (restrictions that do not affect this use):

                                                      over the last ~1 s

  Mean spectral centroid first 60 s: ~1370 Hz — low-mid centred, i.e. warm
  rather than bright, with very little energy above 6 kHz (-34 dB below the
  full-band level), which leaves obvious headroom for UI ticks on top.

Where it matches the brief well:
  * Instrumental, no vocals — nothing competes with on-screen text.
  * The arc is close to the scripted one: near-silent sparse opening, a build
    through the first 14 s, a clear arrival step at 0:14, a sustained driving
    body, and a real ending (it decays to digital silence rather than being
    chopped).
  * Warm, midrange-centred mix; the top end is open enough for UI ticks.
  * No jarring drops: the envelope rises monotonically and holds.
  * 4 min 38 s of material, so the edit has room to choose a better 60 s window
    than the opening if the picture needs it.

Where it is a judgement call and may not fit:
  * The composer describes the opening as "dark" synths. That suits the
    problem/tension half of the ad, but if the brand tone wants brightness from
    the first frame, "Discovery" or "Clear Skies" (above) are safer.
  * The description calls the payoff "adventurous epic". That is bigger and more
    cinematic than the restrained Stripe/Linear-style reference in the brief;
    only listening will settle whether it reads as premium or as trailer music.
  * Steady tempo and no sudden dynamic shifts are supported by the measurements
    (no dips in the envelope), but tempo/BPM was not independently verified.
  * The ending is clean in the file, but the pipeline trims to 60 s, so the cut
    point will need a short manual fade unless the edit lands on a phrase
    boundary — the source does not stop at 1:00.

  verify (Pixabay Content Licence, commercial use, no attribution), and Pixabay
  had good candidate tracks. It was NOT usable from this machine:
    - pixabay.com itself sits behind a Cloudflare bot challenge. Direct curl
      and public CORS/text proxies all returned HTTP 403 "Just a moment...".
      Only a third-party reader proxy (r.jina.ai) could render the pages, and
      it strips the download URL.
    - Every cdn.pixabay.com audio path tried (several filename patterns, with
      and without Referer/Origin/browser headers) returned HTTP 403 with an S3
      body: `<Error><Code>AccessDenied</Code>...`. The same CDN serves the cover
      art fine, so audio delivery is specifically locked down/signed.
    No Pixabay track was downloaded, and no Pixabay licence is relied upon here.

  links redirect to /login ("https://freemusicarchive.org/track/x/download"
  -> "https://freemusicarchive.org/login"). The task forbids signing up, so
  nothing was taken from FMA.
