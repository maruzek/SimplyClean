## The spine

Every good 60-second ad is one idea told once. Ours is the **turnover window**:

> Guests check out at **11:00**. The next ones check in at **15:00**.
> That is four hours to make an apartment spotless — and the host has nobody to call.

The ad never explains the platform. It shows a host living those four hours, then shows the
problem quietly deleting itself. The product is the relief, not the feature list.

**Audience note that shapes every frame:** this is for the host with *one or two* apartments —
not a property-management company. So the ad must never look enterprise. No dashboards full of
charts, no "portfolio view", no "team seats". One property, one cleaner, one confirmation.
Warmth over scale — that is the positioning wedge against the big incumbents.

## Beat 1 — The hook · 0:00.0–0:03.0 · REMOTION · 90 frames

Black. A single low music pulse. A phone screen fades up out of the dark, tilted slightly in 3D
space, held in a pool of soft light. It is the only thing in frame.

Two notifications arrive, stacked, each with a real push-notification feel — a soft haptic
"settle" bounce, not a slide-in:

| Order | Icon | Title | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| +0.6 s | Airbnb red | `New booking` | `Guest arrives tomorrow · 15:00` |
| +1.5 s | amber warning | `Cleaning` | `Not scheduled` |

**The whole ad is in those two lines: a guest is coming, and nobody is coming to clean.**

The second notification is the colour anchor for the film — a warm amber that we will re-use at
the exact moment it is resolved in beat 4.

**Sound cue:** one low pulse on frame 0; a soft tick as each notification lands.

## Beat 2 — The mess · 0:03.0–0:14.0 · LIVE 1 + overlay · 330 frames

Hard cut on the second tick. **Your friend's messy-room clip.**

The footage carries this beat. We add almost nothing — restraint is what makes the relief in
beat 5 land. Only two lines of text, entering low-left, small, quiet:

| In | Out | On-screen text | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:04.5 | 0:08.5 | `11:00 checkout.` | plain |
| 0:06.3 | 0:10.0 | `15:00 check-in.` | plain, stacked under the first |
| 0:09.0 | 0:13.5 | `Four hours.` | heavier weight, slight scale-up |
| 0:11.0 | 0:14.0 | `No cleaning company to call.` | amber, matching beat 1's warning colour |

A subtle vignette plus a very slight desaturation creeps in across the beat, so the frame is
visibly *colder* by 0:14. We will reverse this in beat 5 — the audience will feel the
temperature change without knowing why.

**Crucially: no music swell here.** Keep the bed thin and unresolved so the cut at 0:14 has
somewhere to go.

## Beat 3 — Title card · 0:14.0–0:17.0 · REMOTION · 90 frames

The pressure releases. A light wipe carries us off the mess and onto a clean, warm, off-white
card. The desaturation lifts.

**On-screen text:**

```
        Cleaning that runs
        on your bookings.
```

Logo fades in above the line, small and confident. A thin amber rule draws itself beneath the
text, left to right, in 0.4 s — it is the same amber as the `Not scheduled` warning, now
starting to become a brand colour rather than a problem.

**Sound cue:** the music resolves here. First warm chord of the track. This is the emotional
hinge of the film — spend the cut on it.

## Beat 4 — The connector · 0:17.0–0:36.0 · REMOTION · 570 frames · **HERO**

Nineteen seconds is a third of the ad and it all happens here. This must feel like a real,
beautifully made product — not a cartoon. Design references: Linear, Stripe, Vercel. Generous
whitespace, one accent colour, real typography, honest motion timing.

The camera starts wide on a soft neutral background and pushes in as a browser/app window
scales up into frame and settles. Inside it: the host's single property, and an empty slot where
a booking connection should be.

## Beat 6 — Automation · 0:45.0–0:54.0 · REMOTION · 270 frames

Back to the app window, now in its *connected* state — a direct visual callback to beat 4. A
focused dialog is already sliding up from the bottom of the window:

```
        Want this every time?
        We can schedule cleaning automatically
        around every checkout.

        [ ●———  ]  Auto-schedule after each checkout
        [ ●———  ]  Reuse the same trusted cleaner

                        [   Approve   ]
```

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0:45.0 | Dialog slides up with a soft spring. |
| 0:46.5 | First toggle flips on with a satisfying fill-in animation (0.35 s, custom ease — no linear slide). |
| 0:48.0 | Second toggle flips on. |
| 0:49.5 | Cursor moves to `Approve`, button lifts on hover. |
| 0:50.5 | Click. Button compresses, then resolves into a checkmark. |
| 0:51.5 | A restrained success burst — a single expanding ring, not confetti. Text resolves: `Automated.` |
| 0:52.5 | A quiet line underneath fades in: `We'll handle the rest.` |

**Sound cue:** two UI ticks for the toggles, one for the click, and a warm resolving chord on
`Automated.`

**Why this beat exists:** the user's brief is *ad-hoc* cleaning too, not only recurring. So this
beat must read as an **offer, not a requirement**. The toggles default to off and he opts in —
that is what keeps the ad honest to the one-off customer.

## Beat 7 — Payoff & end card · 0:54.0–1:00.0 · LIVE 3 + end card · 180 frames

**Your friend's third clip:** he sits down, swings his feet onto the table, leans back with his
hands behind his head, and exhales. Held, unhurried. This is the emotional payoff — give it room
and do not clutter it.

The end card animates in over the bottom third at 0:55.5, and the footage is pushed slightly
darker behind it so the type stays legible without a hard scrim:

```
        [ Brand ]

        Turnover cleaning for hosts
        who do it themselves.

        [ Connect your first property → ]

        [brand].com
```

Everything resolves by 0:58.5 and then **holds completely still for the final 1.5 s** — no
drift, no pulse, no fade-out to black. End on the held frame and let the music stop. Cutting to
black at the last second reads as amateur; a held final frame reads as confident.

## Music brief

We need one track. What it must do:

| Section | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Can start from near-silence. Sparse, one or two notes. |
| 0:03–0:14 | Tension without dread. A pulse or tick. **Do not resolve.** |
| 0:14–0:17 | **Arrival.** First warm chord. Nothing before this should feel like a chorus. |
| 0:17–0:36 | Modern, optimistic, driving. Room for UI ticks on top. |
| 0:36–0:45 | Warmest passage of the track. |
| 0:45–0:54 | Confident, moving forward. |
| 0:54–1:00 | Gentle resolution, then **stop** — buttoned off, not faded. |

Practical requirements:

- **Length:** 60 s minimum, or a clean loop point. No vocals that fight the text.
- **Clear midrange:** text is the only storytelling device, so avoid dense mixes there.
- **Rights:** must be a track you can use commercially. This matters more than the track.
- **Deliver:** 320 kbps MP3 or WAV into `assets/music/`.
- The mix will be mastered to **−14 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP** (the streaming/feed target), with
  the music ducked roughly 3–4 dB under the UI ticks so the beats land.

## Copy discipline

Three rules the whole script obeys, worth defending in review:

1. **Never say "platform", "solution", "seamless", "revolutionary", or "AI-powered".** Show it instead.
2. **Concrete beats abstract.** Hours, addresses, badges — not "save time and money".
3. **One idea per card, six words or fewer.** If a line needs a comma to survive, it is two lines.
